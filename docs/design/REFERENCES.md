# Reference board

Nine images the team collected as inspiration (one is a duplicate). They are third-party designs, so the files stay
local in `docs/design/refs/` and are not committed; this page describes them in words. They are inspiration, not
templates. The decisions drawn from them live in [VISUAL_DIRECTION.md](VISUAL_DIRECTION.md).

The functional reference (solarsystemscope.com) is a different thing: it shows what an explorer does, and we deliberately
take nothing from its look.

## What the board has in common

Near-black space. One photographic celestial body as the hero: huge, off-centre, often cropped by the frame. Big,
confident display type for a name. Tiny secondary text. Hairline rules, a visible grid, small numbered indices. A label
over a value. Restraint: the photograph carries the colour.

## Image by image

**01 Moon editorial.** A greyscale Moon fills the right half, cropped by the frame. Left: a short rule, a small
category word, "Moon" in a heavy condensed sans, a short paragraph, a ring-shaped button. Dotted arcs frame the edges.
- Take: the body as a cropped hero; a classification line led by a rule; a circular transport button.
- Leave: decorative dotted arcs (dotted lines mean "illustrative" in our provenance language); vertical text.
- Lands in: inspector plate, inspect framing, time controls.

**02 Mercury, serif on a grid.** A high-contrast Didone "Mercury" over faint vertical column rules; a planet cropped at
the right edge; a "01 —— 03" index and three bottom columns of short facts.
- Take: a Didone for proper names; facts as short labelled columns; structure shown by hairlines.
- Leave: a permanently visible page grid over the universe; numbering on non-sequences.
- Lands in: the Atlas voice, the inspector fact grid.

**03 Solar system selector.** Earth rising from the bottom of the frame as a horizon; a four-column fact row (name,
radius, moons, distance) laid over it; a horizontal strip of planet names with the active one in a pill; previous and
next arrows carrying the neighbouring planets' names.
- Take: the horizon shot; label-over-value facts; the strip with a pill; named previous and next.
- Leave: all-caps headings and labels; even spacing that hides real distances.
- Lands in: the body strip (on a log-distance axis), the cold open, the inspector.

**04 Explore the Moon, callouts.** A red, eclipsed Moon on a fine star field; thin leader lines run from its limb to a
right-aligned column of labels; a vertical tick index.
- Take: leader-line callouts anchored to real features.
- Leave: a red Moon unless an eclipse is actually happening.
- Lands in: feature callouts on bodies.

**05 National Geographic editorial** (07 is the same image). Dark panels framed by hairlines, photographs crossing
panel borders, bold headlines, short dated article lists, a single yellow accent used once per screen, halftone and
hatched textures, a torn-paper transition.
- Take: one accent used sparingly (our brass); hairline-framed panels for dense content; photography that ignores
  panel edges; hatching (repurposed to mark hypothetical regions in labs).
- Leave: print artefacts (torn paper, halftone); the brand's yellow; periods after every headline.
- Lands in: faceplates, the lab, the accent rule.

**06 Sci-fi planet HUD.** Rendered fictional planets with wide, extended display names, coordinates in monospace,
periodic-table tiles for "resources", barcodes, wireframe terrain meshes and dashed connector curves.
- Take: technical numerals in an expanded face (our Experiment voice); coordinates as typography, but real ones;
  composition tiles, but only for measured composition; wireframes, but only for real surfaces such as the spacetime
  embedding.
- Leave: barcodes, invented data, decorative meshes, extended display type for names.
- Lands in: the lab, composition tiles, the relativity lab.

**08 Explore the Moon, multi-page.** Charcoal panels on a star field. A phase strip, a phase name with big numerals and
tiny labels, section titles ending in a slash, a giant numeral overlapping a photograph, numbered photo captions.
- Take: section paths with slashes (our focus-chain breadcrumb); a phase strip as a real time control; big numerals
  with small labels for genuine readouts.
- Leave: giant decorative numerals; numbered captions on unordered sets.
- Lands in: the breadcrumb, the Moon's time controls, the ruler readout.

**09 Orvion technical.** A chrome wordmark, monospaced body copy, a large uppercase statement with a hanging indent,
stat cards with dotted borders and notched corners, wide techno numerals.
- Take: corner brackets (as the selection reticle and lab viewports: "under observation"); wide numerals for results.
- Leave: chrome gradient type; monospaced prose; dotted card borders (dotted is reserved for illustrative content).
- Lands in: the selection reticle, the lab viewport, Experiment numerals.

## What we added that is not on the board

- The Cosmic Ruler as the one bold element, drawn with slide-rule ticks.
- Provenance shown in line texture, after the geological-map convention.
- Réseau crosses from Apollo Hasselblad frames, in lab viewports only.
- Camera framing rules that keep the light physically true.
